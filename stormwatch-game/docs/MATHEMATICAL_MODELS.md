# Mathematical Models

The calculations below describe the current implementation. All authoritative flood and analytics calculations run in Python with NumPy. The client renders returned values and may interpolate display values between snapshots.

## Grid and timestep

The simulation grid is 32×24. Each logical tile is 32×32 pixels; the educational physical assumption is 32×32 metres (1024 m² per cell). Elevation and depth are millimetres, and rainfall/infiltration/drainage rates are millimetres per hour. A step is ten minutes: `dt_hours = 1/6`.

## Rainfall and infiltration

For intensity `R` and impervious ratio `p`, the runoff fraction is `clip(0.20 + 0.75p, 0, 1)`. Gross rain is `R × dt_hours`. The engine partitions rainfall into immediate runoff and infiltration from the remaining portion; infiltration is capped by `infiltration_rate_mm_hr × dt_hours × (1-p)`. The remaining pervious excess joins surface runoff. Roof rain is routed to adjacent flowable cells, while a fully enclosed roof retains its local input.

## Drainage and pumps

Natural ground drainage uses a 2 mm/hour cap on eligible ordinary terrain. Authored inlets provide their configured capacity, and installed pumps add a local capacity field. Pump capacity is attenuated by squared linear distance: `capacity × max(0, 1 - distance/radius)^2`. Removal is capped by available water. Waterways convey laterally rather than acting as an ordinary drainage sink.

## Lateral water flow and barriers

The cell water surface is `elevation_mm + water_depth_mm`. Four orthogonal neighbours are considered. Potential downhill transfer is proportional to the surface-height difference. Transfers are computed from one snapshot, source outflow is capped by a configurable fraction (default 0.25), and equal/opposite deltas are accumulated before any cell is updated. Building cells are obstacles. A river cell uses the same flow rule and may overtop lower terrain; the west boundary can discharge to a fixed sea-level outlet and that loss is counted.

A barrier is attached to an adjacent-cell edge. The crest is the higher adjacent ground elevation plus barrier height. Flow across the edge is blocked while the source water surface is at or below the crest and can pass after overtopping.

## Water balance

The engine tracks rainfall input, infiltration, natural drainage, installed drainage, pump removal, boundary outflow and remaining water volume. Internal cell-to-cell transfers conserve water because their deltas are equal and opposite. Minor floating-point tolerance may appear in volume summaries.

## Synthetic weather and analytics

Weather records use a seeded NumPy generator and storm pulses. Rainfall is spatially related across four map stations; observed station water depth is generated by advancing the Eastbank flood engine, with mapped terrain and drainage. Atmospheric variables depend on storm intensity and changing regimes. Data are synthetic, not a historical record.

- Descriptive statistics include mean, median, min, max and population standard deviation (`ddof=0`).
- A trailing moving average labels each output with the timestamp at the end of its window.
- Regression solves `depth = slope × rainfall + intercept` by NumPy least squares. Constant rainfall safely yields a constant mean-depth fit. The prediction-lab split is chronological; the final 20% is held out.
- Pearson correlation is undefined for constant input and returned as `null`; association is not causation.
- MAE is `mean(abs(actual - predicted))`; RMSE is `sqrt(mean((actual - predicted)^2))`. The moving-average rolling one-step baseline and regression are scored on the same held-out timestamps. Earlier held-out actual depths are available to later rolling baseline predictions.
- The future moving-average forecast repeats its latest observed window mean. Regression extrapolation uses the fitted equation and is not clipped to a physically plausible range.

## Risk and response score

The cell risk index is a weighted display/gameplay heuristic: `100 × (0.56 × clip(depth/100) + 0.20 × clip((2200-elevation)/2200) + 0.14 × clip(rainfall/60) + 0.10 × impervious_ratio)`; building cells are multiplied by 0.85. Risk score is based on F1 overlap between cells the player marked and cells whose risk index is at least 50 or depth at least 25 mm.

The 1000-point response score uses forecast quality (200), risk identification (150), hospital access (200), road accessibility (150), residential protection (150) and resource efficiency (150). Forecast quality uses the best available model MAE, normalized at 40 mm. Efficiency combines remaining budget, pump removal relative to rainfall volume and evacuation effectiveness. The ending thresholds are 750 and 450 points. These are game design metrics, not validated measures of emergency readiness.

## Limitations

This model does not solve shallow-water equations and has no momentum, sub-cell channel geometry, culverts, soil saturation memory, evaporation, tide schedule or calibrated storm forecast. Infiltration is applied to current-step rain, not later standing water. Rescue boats affect evacuation outcomes, not water physics. Refer to `SIMULATION_MODEL.md` and `ANALYTICS_MODEL.md` for implementation-level details.
